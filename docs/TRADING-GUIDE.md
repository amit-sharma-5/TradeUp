# TradeUp Help — Trading in Plain English

New to trading? Start here. This guide explains every idea the app uses, in everyday language. No jargon without a translation.

---

## 1. The one-paragraph version

We watch ~500 of India's biggest, most-traded stocks. We're not hunting for cheap "bargain" stocks — we look for **strong stocks that are pausing for breath and getting ready to continue climbing**. When one breaks out of its pause and starts moving, the app says "this matches the checklist — here's why" and you can place a **pretend trade** (paper trading) to practise. A safety line (**stop-loss**) is set the moment you buy: if the stock falls below it, the trade closes automatically with a small, known loss. Small losses, bigger wins — that's the whole game.

## 2. Words the app uses

**Stock price chart basics**
- A **bar/candle** is one day of trading: where price opened, closed, and the highest/lowest it went (OHLC).
- A **moving average (MA)** is the average price of the last N days, drawn as a smooth line. The 50-day MA is like the stock's "short-term trend line", the 200-day its "long-term one". Price above a rising 200-day MA = healthy uptrend.

**The setup we look for**
- **Uptrend (Stage 2):** the stock has been rising for months, above its rising 200-day MA. We only ever buy in this phase — never in a falling or sideways mess.
- **Base (consolidation):** after rising, a strong stock usually goes quiet for weeks — price wobbles in a narrowing range, like a spring compressing. Different pause shapes have nicknames: **cup with handle**, **flat base**, **VCP** (volatility contraction pattern — each up-down swing smaller than the last: buyers are calmly soaking up supply, not panicking).
- **Pivot (buy point):** the top edge of that pause. When price pushes **above** it, the "breakout" has begun — that's the buy moment.
- **Buy zone:** the small band just above the pivot (about 0–2% above it). Buying here is fine. Chasing a stock that already ran 5% past its pivot is how beginners get stuck.
- **Relative strength (RS):** how hard the stock has been climbing **compared with the whole market** (score 1–99). High RS (≥ 90) = a leader. We want leaders, not laggards.
- **Trend Template:** a master checklist (price above key MAs, MAs rising, RS strong, near a 52-week high, etc.). If a stock fails the checklist, it doesn't matter how good it looks — we skip it.
- **Earnings check:** a real move needs real business growth — rising profit (EPS) and sales. The app flags "fundamentals ✓" when it sees this.

**Protecting your money**
- **Stop-loss:** a pre-set price below your buy. If the stock falls there, the trade closes automatically. It's your seatbelt — decided calmly before buying, not in a panic later.
- **Position size:** how much money to put in one trade — calculated **backwards from the stop**: risk a small fixed amount per trade; the farther the stop, the fewer shares you buy.
- **R-multiple (R):** profit/loss measured in units of your initial risk. Risking ₹1,000 and gaining ₹3,000 = "+3R". A good system wins small R often and occasionally lands 3–5R wins.
- **Chase limit:** if a buy-stop can't fill near the pivot and price has run too far, the order cancels itself. Missing a trade is free; overpaying is not.

**Paper trading**
- Every order you place is **pretend**: the app records it in a **journal** and "fills" it using real market prices, exactly as a broker would. Your money never moves. The journal then honestly tracks each trade to its end — the practice field where losing costs nothing and the lessons are real.

## 3. A typical day with TradeUp

1. **Morning (9–10 AM):** open **Candidates**. Three groups: *Strong Buy* (best setups + strong earnings), *Buy* (good setups), *Watch* (almost ready). Every card tells you **why** in one line. No candidates? Great — doing nothing is a valid trading decision.
2. **Something looks right?** Tap **Trade**. The ticket pre-fills the buy point, the stop-loss, and a suggested quantity based on the risk rule. Read the preview. Confirm — the paper order is placed.
3. **The app works while you don't:** when price reaches your buy point, the order fills; if it runs away first, it cancels with a reason. If the stock later hits your stop, it exits automatically.
4. **Evening / weekend:** open **Journal**. Every trade's timeline shows exactly what happened and why — fills, cancels, stop-outs, exits, with prices and timestamps. This is where the learning happens: the app keeps the receipts so your memory doesn't have to.

## 4. What the app will never do (by design)

- Place a **real** order — everything is paper until you consciously switch that on, much later, with extra confirmations.
- Guarantee anything. Signals are **odds**, not promises: stop-losses get hit, breakouts fail. The point of the system is that many small losses are survivable while the occasional big winner pays for all of them.
- Replace you. The app narrows 500 stocks to a handful and states the reasons; **the decision is always yours.**

*This app is a research and practice tool, not investment advice. Trading real money involves risk of loss.*

## 5. Glossary quick-reference

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| OHLC / bar | One day's open, high, low, close price |
| MA 50/200 | Average price of the last 50/200 days |
| Stage 2 | Confirmed uptrend phase — the only phase we buy in |
| Base / VCP | The quiet pause before a move; each swing tighter than the last |
| Pivot | Top edge of the base — the breakout trigger price |
| Buy zone | Pivot to ~+2%: the sensible buy window |
| RS (1–99) | How strongly the stock beats the overall market |
| EPS / earnings | Company profit per share — the engine behind a big move |
| Stop-loss | Automatic exit if price falls to your safety line |
| Position size | How many shares — derived from your risk and the stop distance |
| R | Profit/loss counted in units of initial risk |
| Paper trading | Practising with pretend money at real prices |
| Journal | The record of every pretend trade and exactly what happened |
